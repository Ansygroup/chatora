WhatsApp API pricing in Egypt (2026): Meta rates and sample costs

Meta prices WhatsApp messages by category and recipient market. This guide uses its Egypt rate card effective 1 October 2026 to show sample arithmetic, including the monthly free service-message allowance. These are not actual customer bills; provider charges, taxes and account settings may add costs.

It also lists 1,000 free service messages each month per business number. These are Meta rates, not a complete provider invoice.

1. What changed on 1 October 2026

If you read a WhatsApp pricing article written before late 2024, it describes a world that no longer exists: one flat fee per 24-hour "conversation". Meta replaced that twice.

  • July 2025 — per message, not per conversation. Every delivered template is billed on its own. Sending a marketing template and then three updates in the same day is now four charges, not one.
  • 1 October 2026 — service replies are billable again. Replies you send inside the 24-hour customer window used to be free and uncapped. Now each business phone number gets 1,000 free service messages per month, and message 1,001 is charged at the market's utility rate. The allowance resets monthly and does not roll over.
  • Check template rules and categories. Do not assume that a template is free because a customer-service window is open; confirm its approved category and the current rate-card treatment.

For a small store, message category and delivered volume both matter. Older guides may use earlier rules, so date-check any estimate and compare it with Meta’s current documentation.

2. The four categories, in plain Arabic

Meta puts every message you send into exactly one category, and the category sets the price for the recipient's country. There is no negotiation and no way to "pick" a cheaper one: the template you submit is what gets classified.

  • Marketing — promotions and offers. The October 2026 Egypt card lists $0.0644 per delivered message; check current volume and account rules.
  • Utility — qualifying updates about a customer’s transaction, such as an order or shipping update. The Egypt card lists $0.0036.
  • Authentication — qualifying one-time passwords and login codes. The Egypt card lists $0.0036; an international authentication rate may apply in eligible markets. Check the recipient market’s current rate.
  • Service — customer-service replies under Meta’s current rules. The card lists 1,000 free service messages per business number each month, then $0.0036 in Egypt.

Meta’s current rate card defines which messages are billable and how delivery is counted. Check the latest documentation and compare delivered-message counts with your provider’s invoice rather than assuming that a sent count equals a billed count.

3. Three illustrative calculations

These examples use the Egypt card effective 1 October 2026 and assume all listed messages are delivered and correctly classified. They exclude provider fees, taxes, discounts and other business costs; they are arithmetic examples, not customer bills.

Example 1 — service messages. Assume one business phone number has 12,000 delivered service replies in a month. After 1,000 free messages, 11,000 × $0.0036 = $39.60 in Meta fees. Actual counts and other fees vary.
Example 2 — marketing messages. If 10,000 marketing messages to eligible recipients are delivered to Egyptian numbers, 10,000 × $0.0644 = $644 at Meta’s listed rate. This is a Meta-fee calculation, not a platform charge or complete invoice.
Example 3 — a cart follow-up. If 800 carts each receive two delivered utility messages and the approved category and content qualify, 1,600 × $0.0036 = $5.76 in Meta fees. At a $30 order and 30% gross margin, a hypothetical 4% recovery means $288 gross profit before other costs—50 times this message-fee example, not a full ROI estimate.

These examples do not establish a typical share of revenue or a typical category mix. Use your own delivered-message counts, approved categories, margin and provider invoice to estimate the costs and results for your business.

4. Other rate-card rules and eligibility checks

Meta’s pricing can include market-specific rules, eligibility conditions and exceptions for certain entry points. Window lengths and charges have changed over time, and vendor documentation may describe them differently. Check Meta’s current pricing page and the specific account terms before including an exception or free window in a budget.

If you use ads that open WhatsApp, confirm which messages qualify for any current no-charge window, for how long, and whether follow-up templates are included. Do not assume that every message in the conversation is free; include provider charges and the ad spend in your estimate.

5. How to compare provider billing

Meta’s rates are public, but a provider invoice may use different display and billing terms. For example, ManyChat’s guide dated 7 October 2026 lists Egypt marketing at $0.0740 per message; Meta’s card effective 1 October lists $0.0644. The difference alone does not establish its cause or prove a particular markup. Ask the provider for the applicable rate, included fees and a sample invoice.

Questions to ask any vendor before connecting WhatsApp:

  • Which message rate and provider fees apply to my market and account, and where are they documented?
  • Are any fees per message, usage-based or a flat monthly amount, and can they vary by volume?
  • Can I see cost per message inside the dashboard before I hit send, or only on the invoice?
  • If I leave, do I keep the phone number and the template history?

6. Seven ways to cut the bill

  1. Let the customer start the chat. Inbound is free. A comment-to-DM or click-to-chat flow pushes the first message onto their side of the ledger.
  2. Keep the 1,000 free service messages for support. Don't burn them on upsell scripts — that is a marketing cost wearing a support costume.
  3. Segment by recency, not by size. 10,000 messages to people who bought last month beats 30,000 to everyone who ever touched the list.
  4. Send receipts as utility, not marketing. "Your order shipped" is utility at $0.0036; a "we miss you!" card is marketing at $0.0644 and people report it.
  5. Use the channels your business already supports. Email or another channel may suit some updates, but check its consent, service and pricing rules; do not assume a tool integration is live.
  6. Monitor quality and delivery. Follow Meta’s current account warnings, review complaints and delivery outcomes, and pause or adjust activity when required by policy.
  7. Ask for the reconciliation. Meta's own billing shows the number of delivered messages per category. Compare it monthly against your platform's count; variance usually means templates were classified differently than you assumed.

Frequently asked, briefly

Is the WhatsApp Business API free?

Meta’s published Cloud API pricing is message-based for the listed categories and markets, but third-party providers may charge subscriptions or usage fees. Check the current rate card and the provider terms for your account.

Did service replies stop being free?

Yes, on 1 October 2026, after being free since November 2024. Each business phone number now gets 1,000 free service messages per month; the rest are charged at the utility rate for that country.

Is a BSP mandatory?

A third-party business provider is not always required for a Cloud API setup, but direct onboarding still involves Meta’s current account, permissions and technical requirements. Check the official documentation and choose an implementation path that fits your team.

Do these numbers expire?

They can. The figures above were checked against Meta’s published card on 28 October 2026; rates and eligibility can change, so revisit the official source before budgeting.
