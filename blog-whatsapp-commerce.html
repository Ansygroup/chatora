WhatsApp commerce in MENA: the 2026 playbook

WhatsApp can support customer service, order updates and commerce conversations in Egypt, Saudi Arabia and the UAE. This guide covers channel setup, message fees, customer consent and practical workflow checks; results and availability depend on your business and provider.

1. Official API setup: permissions and checks

WhatsApp Cloud API access and message pricing are governed by Meta’s current documentation, while third-party platforms may charge their own subscriptions or usage fees. Requirements vary by account, provider and market. Chatora has no live WhatsApp integration in its current preview, so it cannot promise onboarding time or a working setup.

  1. Complete any business verification Meta requires and provide the current supporting documents for your account.
  2. Confirm your phone number’s eligibility and the current coexistence, registration or migration rules before changing its setup.
  3. Submit a display name and follow the current review requirements shown in Meta’s onboarding flow.
  4. Check when a pre-approved template is required outside the active customer-service window.
  5. Collect and retain customer consent according to the channel’s policies and applicable law.

Do not assume that a verification badge is the same as API access or that a paid package is required to obtain one. Check the current Meta guidance and the exact service a vendor promises.

2. The fees nobody explains

Since 1 October 2026 the numbers changed again: service replies are billable after 1,000 a month per number. The full rate card, with three worked bills, is in WhatsApp API pricing in 2026.

Two different costs get mixed together in every WhatsApp article:

  • Platform fees — what your software provider charges. Subscription, seat or usage terms vary; Chatora has no published subscription price.
  • Meta message fees — Meta’s rates depend on delivered-message category and recipient market. Its October 2026 Egypt card lists $0.0644 for marketing and $0.0036 for utility, authentication and service; it also provides 1,000 free service messages per business number per month. Check the effective card and current policy before budgeting.

Use Meta’s current rate card to estimate message charges, then add the provider’s own subscription or usage terms and taxes. Message category is determined by Meta’s rules and template content—not by choosing the cheapest label. Compare the estimate with your actual invoices.

3. Cash on delivery: the real problem

Cash-on-delivery orders can create confirmation and failed-delivery work for retailers. Whether messaging helps depends on the courier process, customer consent and the reason an order fails; measure your own baseline before choosing a workflow.

  • Offer a clear confirmation option before dispatch, such as confirm or cancel, where your operational system supports it. Track the response rate in your own account.
  • Share accurate courier and tracking information through a supported channel, if the customer expects those updates.
  • Provide a rescheduling route after a failed attempt and record whether the order is delivered, cancelled or returned.

No delivery-recovery percentage is claimed here. Compare failed-delivery and return outcomes over a consistent period, account for other operational changes, and include messaging and courier costs.

4. Broadcasts that don't burn your list

Unwanted or poorly targeted broadcasts can lead to negative feedback. Use a permission-based list and follow the current Meta and local requirements:

  • Segment thoughtfully. Use relevant purchase or inquiry context only where you have permission and a suitable purpose.
  • Set a measured frequency. There is no universal weekly cap for every business; follow the current rules and monitor opt-outs and complaints.
  • Record the opt-in and explain what kinds of updates the customer is agreeing to receive.
  • Monitor account quality in Meta’s current business tools and follow the instructions shown for your account.
  • Provide a clear way to stop messages and honor opt-outs promptly across your sending systems.

5. Writing for Arabic properly

Machine-translated formal Arabic reads like a government form. People in Cairo message in Egyptian dialect; in Riyadh and Jeddah, Gulf dialect; in Casablanca, a French-Arabic mix. Practical rules:

  • Use short sentences and the customer's own words back to them.
  • Write prices in local currency with the right digits and separators.
  • Right-to-left layouts break naive templates — test every message on an actual phone.
  • Emojis are punctuation in this market. One or two, not seven.

Chatora’s site and workspace preview support Arabic and English interfaces. Live AI replies, dialect detection and production message performance are not enabled or benchmarked.

6. Launch checklist

  1. Business verified on Meta, phone number ready.
  2. Three templates approved: order confirmation, dispatch notice, delivery follow-up.
  3. Opt-in keyword flow ("SEND" / "ابعت") published.
  4. COD confirmation flow with one-tap confirm.
  5. Human handoff rule for complaints and refunds.
  6. Weekly broadcast segment defined.
  7. Quality rating monitoring on a recurring calendar reminder.
